Hi! This is Dragons!, a cozy phone game where you raise dragons, race them and watch every race like a live TV sport. Everything you'll see is the real game, drawn by its own engine.

The idea

Raise them. Race them. Watch them fly.

A Tamagotchi you take to the races: a dragon of your own, grown from an egg, flying in a league that's broadcast like a real sport.

Raise

Warm an egg, hatch a kid, feed it, play with it, watch it grow into a teen and an adult.

Race

Kids, Teen and Main Event leagues, seasons, divisions and an awards ceremony at the end.

Breed

Bring adults to the Soul Altar for a new egg that inherits their looks, or surprises you.

Three loops. Raise: daily care, a few minutes at a time. Race: the main event, a ranked season per league. Breed: the long game, new dragons from your best ones.

Every dragon is one of a kind

2.9 trillion

possible combinations of colours, parts and rare traits, before growing up and before the shape genes that make each body unique.

No artist draws them: every dragon grows from a seed, and the rarer the combination, the more it's worth showing off.

32 colours for the scales, wings and underside, five heads, five headgears, seven feet, six tail tips and more. That's 2.9 trillion discrete combinations, and the body proportions are continuous on top of that. No two dragons are alike.

Coat markings

Same palette, new coats

On top of the colours, one dragon in four wears markings: leopard spots, zebra stripes or cow patches, each tinted to suit its scales.

Mix and match

Heads and horns

Five heads, from a toothy one to a viper, five headgears, from swept horns to antlers, and seven eye colours, gold the commonest.

Mix and match

Tails, wings and legs

Six tail tips, rare hind wings, four leg shapes, plus seven kinds of feet and five wing fingers. Every part is generated, so a new part works on every dragon right away.

Expressions

Every feeling shows on its face

Eyelids smile, droop and close, the eyes blink and glance around, and the mouth roars and pants. The mood comes straight from how the dragon is doing.

A happy dragon smiles with its lower lids, a sad one droops them, a tired or hungry one gets heavy eyelids. They blink every few seconds, glance about, and close their eyes to sleep. In races they roar, snap and pant. Owners read their dragon's mood at a glance, without a single number.

Growing up

Kid, teen, adult

Every dragon hatches as a chubby kid, shoots up into a gangly teen and grows into a full adult. Care decides how strong it gets.

Kids all share the same round baby face; teens get their own head and big eyes; adults the full horns. Kids race at 82% of an adult's speed and teens at 90%, like 50cc, 100cc and 150cc.

Five elements

Fire, nature, earth, storm, water

Every dragon breathes one. Each beats the next, like rock, paper, scissors, so who you bring to a race matters.

Breath arrives with the teen. In a race a hit slows the rival, knocks it back and dazes it, twice as hard when it's super effective.

Day one

Say your name, pick your kid

  • The stable hand walks you through it in a 3D meadow.
  • You ride every dragon you own: tell the stable hand your name.
  • Three kids stand in a row. Pick one to raise, and get a gift egg too.
The first five minutes: you say your name, you pick your first dragon, and you're off to your first race in the Kids League.

Home

The stable yard

  • Your dragons live in a 3D yard: drag to walk around them, pinch to get close.
  • Pick another one and they swap places: one flies off, the next glides in.
  • Room for six eggs and dragons, so every slot is a choice.
The stable is the landing screen. It shows the dragon in view with its name plate, its mood and the one thing it needs most right now.

Eggs

Warm it, hatch it, grow it

Warming an egg brings hatching closer and makes the kid a little stronger. Hatching and growing up are little shows of their own.

An egg incubates for eight hours; warming it once an hour skips ahead. Kids become teens after four hours and adults after eight more. When a stage is done the dragon waits for you to evolve it. As a teen, it shows you its breath for the first time.

Tamagotchi care

A few minutes, a few times a day

  • Kids need feeding, play and cleaning.
  • Teens add exercise, adults affection.
  • Needs drop slowly, even while the game is closed, and reminders on the phone tell you when.
  • Good care makes a dragon stronger: a 1-star hatchling reaches 5 stars in about three days. Nothing ever dies.
Care is the heartbeat of the game. The yard shows one care tile for the lowest need, and each one opens a mini-game. Care also grows the bond, and the bond never fades.

Mini-games

Every care action is a little game

Rub the egg in circles to keep it glowing just right. Toss snacks for the kid to catch. Play is volleyball or fetch: keep the ball up, or throw the stick and watch it chase.

Mini-games

Scrub, rinse, work out, groom

Cleaning is scrub, then rinse. Teens add a workout to the beat, and adults like grooming: find the itchy spot. Grooming builds the bond the most.

Mini-games

In motion

Short, touchy and satisfying: a care session is under a minute.

Get to know it

Profile, traits and stats

Each racing stat comes from the body: a long torso for top speed, big wings for acceleration, a long tail for handling, bulk for weight and big horns for breath. You can see what your dragon is built for just by looking at it.

Life goes on

Naps, news and reminders

The game keeps going while you're away: races finish, eggs get ready, needs drop. When you come back, a sheet tells you what happened, and each line opens the screen that deals with it.

The Soul Altar

New eggs from your best dragons

  • Bring two to six adults to the stone circle. More parents, better odds of an egg.
  • Every part and colour comes whole from one parent, or mutates into something none of them has.
  • A metallic coat passes down too.
Two parents give a 36% chance of an egg, six give 90%. The lineage screen shows where every trait came from, and celebrates every mutation.

The ritual

A show every time

The custodian raises his staff, the parents land round the ring and breathe at the altar together, and their breaths meet in fireworks. Then an egg flashes onto the stone, or the lights fade and he consoles you.

Leagues

Three leagues, three divisions

  • Kids League, Teen League and the Main Event: every race is for one age.
  • Seasons of 16 races in Bronze, Silver and Gold. The top two go up, the bottom two go down.
  • Seven rival stables per division, each with its own riders and dragons.
Age leagues keep every race fair. Your stable climbs from Bronze to Gold, and the Gold champion is the league champion.

Race day

Pick your dragon, meet the field

  • Eight starters: your dragon and seven rivals, in gate order.
  • Form is shown before the race: a rival can be in perfect shape, or off.
  • A race tires a dragon, so a season is a few check-ins, not a grind.
One tap to enter. The favourite is marked, but upsets happen: the favourite wins about one race in five.
This is a race. Nobody is holding the camera: a director plans every cut as the race happens, looking three seconds ahead for the overtake, the fall and the photo finish.

The broadcast

3, 2, 1, Go

Every race opens low on the grid, then the field crouches and leaps off the ground, kicking up dust.

How a dragon leaves the ground depends on its legs and its weight, so even the start tells you something about the racers.

Director's cut

Directed like television

Trackside, fly-by and onboard shots, with speed lines, vapour and blur. Valleys with forests, rivers and a real-scale castle, and canyons with arches and spires.

Breath

Strategy in the air

Slipstream slingshots a chaser past, a clean pass through a gate gives a burst, and a well-timed breath knocks a rival back.

No stamina bar: a dragon flies at its top speed unless something slows it down. The race is about the line and the timing.

Autopilot or the reins

Watch it, or fly it yourself

  • Every race starts on Autopilot: sit back and watch the broadcast.
  • Take the reins any time: one thumb to steer, one button to breathe.
  • A rider who flies the line well beats Autopilot. Hand it back whenever you like.
Racing is never forced: it's a show you can watch, or a game you can play. Leave mid-race and it flies on by itself, and you get the result when you're back.

Riding

Chase cam or the rider's eyes

Two cameras while riding: a chase camera behind and above, or right through the rider's goggles.

The finish

Touchdown, then the podium

The winner glides in to land in slow motion, the ground shaking and the dust flying. Then the results: times, gaps, points and the standings.

End of the season

The awards ceremony

  • Every season ends on the podium: the cup comes down over the champion and confetti falls.
  • Medals for the top three, trophies for champions, and a prize egg to raise next.
  • Every past ceremony can be replayed.
This closes the loop: the prize egg starts the next dragon, and the cycle begins again.

Design tokens

Cozy by design

One small set of tokens drives every screen: a warm palette, one rounded typeface in three weights, soft radii and soft warm shadows, no harsh borders. Playtesters picked this look over the original dark racing one.
